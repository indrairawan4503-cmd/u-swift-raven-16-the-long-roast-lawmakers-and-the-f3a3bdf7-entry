# Versions — variant C: THE RULE THEY WROTE

Concept (one idea, iterated v1–v8): the lawmaker authors a rule requiring
every filing in PDF, then their OWN filing bounces off their own system
because they sent a Pages file, and they ask an intern to convert it.
Skeleton from the winner playbook: [named rule they wrote] → [applied
literally to the wrong target: themselves] → [the powerful person gets
inverted]. Punch lands on the last few words ("convert it into a PDF
for him").

- **v1 — 215 chars.** "The senator who wrote the law requiring every filing in PDF spent the morning emailing his own disclosure as a .pages file, and after his own system bounced it three times, he asked the intern to convert it for him." Base concept works, but far under the 260–320 budget — a thin joke loses pairwise on bite first.
- **v2 — 245 chars.** Named the rule ("Mandatory PDF Filing Act"), swapped "system" for "his own portal", added "unsupported format". Still under budget; the authority setup is still weak.
- **v3 — 258 chars.** "signed it into law" + "watched his own portal bounce it" — stronger authority image, but still 2 chars short of the 260 floor.
- **v4 — 302 chars.** Added "on live television" (public authority, bigger fall) and the intern "earns twelve dollars an hour" (the power inversion: the powerful man depends on the cheapest person in the room). In budget; the punch is getting sharp.
- **v5 — 318 chars.** Intern becomes "twenty-year-old" — the age gap sharpens the inversion (veteran lawmaker vs kid). In budget, but ".pages" still carries a second period.
- **v6 — 316 chars.** "emailed his own disclosure form as" → "filed his own disclosure form as" — tighter verb, "filed" echoes the filing rule itself. Still two periods (".pages" + terminal).
- **v7 — 327 chars (rejected).** Tried closing on "convert it into a PDF for him" (the rule's own words as the punch) but overshot the 320 cap — dropped.
- **v8 — 318 chars (WINNER).** Kept the v7 punch, dropped "quietly" to fit, and wrote "Pages file" instead of ".pages file" so the text has exactly ONE period (the terminal one) — zero risk on the one-sentence gate. Final text:

> The lawmaker who authored the Mandatory PDF Filing Act signed it into law on live television, then filed his own disclosure form as a Pages file, watched his own portal bounce it three times for unsupported format, and asked the twenty-year-old intern who earns twelve dollars an hour to convert it into a PDF for him.

Why v8 won: full 318/320 budget; named rule + named format + named failure count ("three times") + named wage ("twelve dollars an hour") — four specific details, no worn cliche (no sticky note, no printed email, no "what is the cloud"); the rule they wrote rejects them, and the last words are the rule's own demand turned back on its author.
